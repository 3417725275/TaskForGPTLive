IFC (Industry Foundation Classes) is the sector-specific neutral interoperability format for AEC (Architecture, Engineering, Construction). Created in 1996 by the IAI (now buildingSMART International), IFC addresses the need for structured exchange of digital building models between heterogeneous BIM software — Revit, ArchiCAD, Tekla, Allplan, Microstation. Its EXPRESS schema derived from STEP encodes geometry, business properties, and spatial relationships of a building in an open, non-proprietary file. ISO 16739-1:2024 formalizes the IFC4.3 version, extending coverage to civil infrastructure.

In 2026, IFC is the only internationally recognized open BIM exchange standard: no competing format covers the same multi-discipline scope. Unlike STEP AP242, which targets mechanical B-Rep exchange with PMI/GD&T, IFC specifically addresses AEC data — spatial structure, MEP systems, family properties, and classifications. Adoption is steadily growing: over 15 European countries mandate IFC on public contracts, and France formalized the requirement through its Plan BIM 2022. The IFC4.3 extension to infrastructure (roads, bridges, tunnels, railways) accelerates its spread beyond the building sector.

The Construction, Architecture, Structural Engineering, MEP, and Facility Management sectors use IFC on a daily basis. The main interoperability challenge remains the incomplete mapping of curved B-Rep geometries from mechanical CAD, IfcPropertySet losses between vendors, and geometric degradation of IFC2x3 exports into CSG or faceted polyhedra. This page answers the recurring question from BIM managers: how to guarantee the geometric and semantic fidelity of an IFC file across a multi-vendor software chain?

This guide covers IFC technical specifications, CAD and BIM software compatibility, conversion pitfalls, and best practices for leveraging IFC without data loss at every project stage.

#### When to Use IFC?

- Best fit: Multi-discipline BIM exchange between Revit, ArchiCAD, Tekla, Allplan, and Microstation. Delivery of digital building models on public contracts mandating openBIM (European Directive, France Plan BIM 2022). Long-term building archival per ISO 19650 requirements. 4D construction coordination and Facility Management across the entire building lifecycle.

- Avoid if: The need involves exchanging mechanical B-Rep parts with PMI/GD&T — use STEP AP242 instead. IFC is not designed for numerical simulation (FEA/CFD), parametric feature transfer, or high-precision NURBS surface exchange outside the building context.

- Sources and migration: The main source applications are Revit (.rvt), ArchiCAD (.pln), Tekla (.tekla), and Allplan (.ndw). To stay within the Autodesk ecosystem without openBIM constraints, native Revit retains more design intelligence. For associated 2D drawings, DXF/DWG remains the de facto standard.

## IFC Technical Specifications

### Geometric Representation

- Hybrid modeling: CSG (Constructive Solid Geometry), parametric sweeps (swept solids), extruded profiles, faceted polyhedra. IFC4 adds full B-Rep (Advanced B-Rep with NURBS surfaces).

- Precision: Tolerance defined by IfcGeometricRepresentationContext.Precision (typically 1.0E-5 m). Parametric profiles (I, L, T, U, Z) encode exact dimensions (width, web and flange thickness).

- Tessellation: IFC4 introduces IfcTriangulatedFaceSet and IfcPolygonalFaceSet for compact meshes. An alternative to B-Rep for geometries imported from mesh formats.

- Encoding: IFC-SPF (.ifc, Part 21 text) is the most compact and readable format. IFC-XML (.ifcXML) offers XML tool interoperability but generates files 300–400% larger. IFC-ZIP (.ifcZIP) compresses IFC-SPF by 70–90%.

### Embedded Data

- Spatial structure: Hierarchy IfcProject → IfcSite → IfcBuilding → IfcBuildingStorey → IfcSpace. Decomposition and aggregation via IfcRelAggregates and IfcRelContainedInSpatialStructure.

- Business properties: No mechanical PMI (GD&T). Business data is carried through IfcPropertySet, IfcQuantitySet, and classifications (IfcClassificationReference). Each element carries its standardized Pset_* properties.

- Appearance: Materials (IfcMaterial, IfcMaterialLayerSet), colors (IfcStyledItem), and textures (IFC4). Layers are not managed: filtering uses IFC element types and MVDs.

IFC (Industry Foundation Classes) is the open interoperability standard for the AEC (Architecture, Engineering, Construction) sector. Developed since 1994 by buildingSMART International and standardized as ISO 16739-1:2024, IFC structures building data — geometry, properties, spatial relationships — in an EXPRESS schema derived from STEP. This neutral format eliminates dependency on proprietary native formats (.rvt, .pln, .dgn) and ensures reliable exchange between heterogeneous software.

IFC holds a dominant position in the global BIM ecosystem, covering architecture, structural engineering, MEP (Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing), Facility Management, and construction planning. Several European governments (Denmark, Finland, Norway, France via the Plan BIM 2022) mandate its use on public contracts. IFC4 introduced full B-Rep support, addressing the main geometric limitation of IFC2x3. IFC4.3 (2024) extends coverage to civil infrastructure: roads, bridges, railways, and tunnels.

IFC interoperability remains a technical challenge. Implementation gaps between vendors (MVD mapping, IfcPropertySet handling, geometric fidelity at export) cause recurring data losses. Unlike formats derived from a single geometric kernel (Parasolid, ACIS), IFC must handle heterogeneous representations (CSG, sweeps, B-Rep). IFC conversion requires a rigorous control chain: topological healing, semantic validation, and visual verification.
