## What is Computer-Aided Design (CAD)? Meaning and definition

CAD is a design process that uses software or tools to create, analyze and optimize technical drawings, 2D/3D models and detailed representations of systems prior to production. Often used by engineers or designers, it replaces manual drafting and complex calculations, enables rapid concept development and improves design accuracy.

## What is Computer-Aided Engineering (CAE)? Meaning and definition

CAE is a design and engineering process that uses advanced software or tools to simulate the real-world performance of products before manufacturing. It enables engineers to predict product behavior under different conditions and to validate designs virtually before physical prototypes are created, reducing development time and costs.

## What is the difference between CAD and CAE?

CAD is used to create 2D/3D product models, and CAE is used to simulate and test them before production. CAD and CAE are both design methods that use software or tools for product design and modeling, and they are often used in coordination with each other.

## CAD vs CAE software: How do they differ?

Even though CAD and CAE software are often used together, there are instances when your workflow may call for one tool over the other. Let’s break down the unique capabilities and benefits of CAD and CAE software.

### CAD software

Capabilities of CAD software include:

- 2D/3D modeling

- Technical drawings

- Model-Based Definition (MBD)

- Building Information Modeling (BIM)

Benefits of CAD software include:

- Easy understanding of the shape and properties of an object designed in 3D

- Rapid concept development and visualization

- Design changes can be made easily

- Improved accuracy and precision

Learn more about CAD software.

### CAE software

Capabilities of CAE tools include:

- Finite element analysis (FEA)

- Computational fluid dynamics (CFD)

- Multi-disciplinary design optimization (MDO)

Benefits of using CAE tools include:

- Fewer physical prototypes needed

- Reduced potential for errors

- Visibility into how changes to product design affect performance

- Reduced costs and time to market

- Accelerated virtual prototyping

Learn more about Computer-Aided Engineering.

## What about CAM? How does its technology compare to CAD and CAE?

CAM, or Computer-Aided Manufacturing, is another design method that uses software to automate the manufacturing process. It does this by transforming digital designs into instructions, or g-code, that guide computer-controlled machinery to produce physical components.

Often, CAD, CAE and CAM tools are used together to support a complete digital workflow. CAD is used to create an accurate product model, CAE is used to analyze a product’s behavior before production, and CAM ensures the product is manufactured efficiently with precision and accuracy.

### Preparing for natural disasters with BIM and 3D modeling

Japan is no stranger to natural disasters and is particularly vulnerable to flooding, landslides, typhoons, earthquakes and tsunamis. Civil engineering firm Pacific Consultants aims to contribute to landslide prevention for Japanese residents by designing what are known as “check dams,” which directly stop mud and landslides.

The company has adopted the 3DEXPERIENCE platform on the cloud for its building information modeling (BIM) and 3D modeling capabilities to see its check dam designs come to life in the greatest detail possible. No longer do they have to manually update designs, and complex calculations that used to take an hour now take three seconds. The result? Pacific Consultants can develop new infrastructure designs at a pace that keeps up with the unpredictable nature of natural disasters, helping Japan better protect its residents when emergencies arise.
